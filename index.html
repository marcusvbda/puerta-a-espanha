Why Spain?

Six reasons Spain is the smartest path to Europe

Other countries ask you to wait 5 to 10 years for citizenship. For Ibero-American nationals, Spain is one of the fastest routes in the whole European Union — and every non-EU citizen has several legal ways in.

⚡

Fast citizenship route

Nationals of Ibero-American countries — Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, Brazil, Peru and others — can apply after 2 years of legal and continuous residence instead of the usual 10.

🇪🇺

EU passport & free movement

As a Spanish citizen you can live, work and study in any EU country. Ibero-American nationals can keep their original nationality; others may need to give it up — check your case.

🏥

Public healthcare

Spain has a universal public health system. Legal residents who work or pay into the system can use it.

🛡️

Safety

Spain is considered one of the safer countries in Europe, with walkable cities and a relaxed street life day and night.

☀️

Quality of life & climate

Mild winters, long summers, beaches and mountains. Life happens outside, and the food is fantastic.

🗣️

Culture & language

Spanish is the official language of most of Latin America, so many Ibero-American immigrants arrive already speaking it — and don't need the DELE exam. Portuguese speakers, like Brazilians, learn Spanish fast because the languages are very close. Latin culture, family values and a warm welcome.

Path to citizenship

From visa to EU passport

Spanish Civil Code, article 22. The years must be legal, continuous and immediately before your application: the 2 (or 10) years right before the day you apply.

10

years — the general rule for most other non-EU nationals (e.g. Morocco, India, USA, China…)

2

years — nationals of Ibero-American countries (Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Cuba, Dominican Republic, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Mexico, Nicaragua, Panama, Paraguay, Peru, Uruguay, Venezuela…), plus Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea, Portugal and people of Sephardic origin

1

year — for example, if you have been married to a Spanish citizen for 1 year and live together

🧳 Can I come as a tourist first?

Many Latin Americans don't need a visa for short visits (up to 90 days). Some permits can be requested from inside Spain while you are there legally as a visitor — for example, Digital Nomad, Highly Qualified and Entrepreneur permits, and, since the 2025 regulation, Student permits too. Others must be requested at the Spanish consulate in your country — for example, the Non-Lucrative Visa and most work visas. Time as a tourist never counts for citizenship, and if you stay longer than 90 days without a permit, you become irregular.

Month 0

Get your residence permit and register your address

Empadronamiento (registering at the town hall) is mandatory for everyone who lives in Spain, whatever your nationality or visa. You need it for healthcare, schools and renewals. For citizenship, your clock starts on the date your first residence permit is granted, not the day you arrive.

Year 1

Keep your residence valid

Renew your permit on time and avoid long trips outside Spain. Your years must be continuous.

Year 1–2

Pass the CCSE and, if needed, the DELE A2

Everyone takes the CCSE, a test on the Spanish Constitution and culture. If Spanish is not an official language in your country (for example Brazil, the Philippines, Morocco or India), you also need the DELE A2 Spanish exam. Both are run by Instituto Cervantes. Exemptions: nationals of Spanish-speaking countries don't need the DELE; children under 18 and people who completed compulsory school (ESO) in Spain may be exempt from the CCSE too.

Year 2 (Ibero-American) · Year 10 (most others)

Apply for nationality

Submit your application to the Ministry of Justice with a clean criminal record and proof of "good civic conduct". Processing can take a long time.

After approval

Oath and Spanish passport 🎉

Swear loyalty to the King and the Constitution, register in the Civil Registry and get your DNI and passport. Ibero-American nationals (and Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea, Portugal and Sephardic Jews) can keep their original nationality; most other non-EU nationals need to give it up.

⚠️ What does NOT count (or has caveats)

  • Time living in Spain irregularly (without a permit) does not count. If you get papers through arraigo, the clock starts when the permit is granted.
  • Tourist stays do not count as residence, even if you later get a permit from inside Spain.
  • Long absences from Spain can break "continuous" residence.
  • Your permit must be valid on the day you apply.
  • Student time: since the 2025 immigration regulation, student stays are residence permits and count as legal residence.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Who can get citizenship after only 2 years?
Nationals of Ibero-American countries (Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Peru and others), plus Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea, Portugal and people of Sephardic origin. Everyone else applies under the general 10-year rule (or 1 year for some family cases). The years must be legal, continuous residence immediately before the application. After you apply, the government still needs time to process the case.
Do I need to take a Spanish exam?
Everyone takes the CCSE (Constitution and culture). You also need the DELE A2 (Spanish language) unless Spanish is an official language in your country — that covers most of Latin America and Equatorial Guinea.
Will I lose my current nationality?
Ibero-American nationals, and people from Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea, Portugal or of Sephardic origin, can keep their original nationality. Most other non-EU nationals need to formally give it up when they take the oath — check the rules of your own country too, since some do not allow dual nationality either way.
Can I enter as a tourist and apply from Spain?
Some visas — like Digital Nomad, Highly Qualified and Entrepreneur, and Student since 2025 — can be requested from inside Spain if you are there legally as a visitor. Others, like Non-Lucrative and most work visas, must be requested at a Spanish consulate in your country. Either way, time spent as a tourist never counts toward citizenship.
I'm not from Latin America. Can I still move to Spain?
Yes. Every visa on this site is open to any non-EU citizen — remote worker, student, professional, founder or family member. The only difference is the citizenship timeline: 2 years for Ibero-American nationals (and a few other groups), 10 years for most others.
Can I still get a Golden Visa?
No. Spain abolished the Golden Visa (residence by property investment) in April 2025.
Can I work with a Non-Lucrative Visa?
No. This visa is for people who live from savings or passive income. If you want to work, look at the Digital Nomad, work or student options.
Does time as a tourist count?
No. Only legal residence with a valid permit counts. Tourist stays and periods without papers do not count toward citizenship.
